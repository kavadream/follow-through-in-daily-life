# Sources and Further Reading

Sources for this edition were checked on November 2, 2026. The following correspond to research discussions in the text; this is not a recommended reading list claiming that every work has been studied in full. The actual reading scope is stated honestly. No full papers, figures, tables, or scales are reproduced. The text does not turn research effect sizes into an individual's probability of success or present associations as universal causal relationships.

## S1: Opportunities for Action and Specific Plans

Webb, T. L., & Sheeran, P. (2007). How do implementation intentions promote goal attainment? A test of component processes. *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 43*(2), 295–302. DOI: 10.1016/j.jesp.2006.02.001.

[Journal page](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2006.02.001) · [University of Sheffield repository](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/10753/)

Discussed in Chapter 3. Actual reading scope: public abstracts and bibliographic information from the journal and institutional repository, not full-text study. Used for the research background on specific situations and action plans. The lunch-break, reading, and file-practice examples were devised for this book and were not experimental tasks in the study. This book does not present “implementation intentions” as its own theory.

## S2: Retrieval Activities in Learning

Roediger, H. L., III, & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science, 17*(3), 249–255. DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x.

[PubMed abstract and bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/)

Discussed in Chapter 7. Actual reading scope: the full public abstract and bibliographic information on PubMed, not full-text study. The study used particular materials, participants, and testing conditions. This book uses it to distinguish familiarity from delayed recall, not to promote a single method for every learning task. The specific note-taking and practice arrangements are this book's suggestions.

## S3: Monitoring Progress

Harkin, B., Webb, T. L., Chang, B. P. I., Prestwich, A., Conner, M., Kellar, I., Benn, Y., & Sheeran, P. (2016). Does monitoring goal progress promote goal attainment? A meta-analysis of the experimental evidence. *Psychological Bulletin, 142*(2), 198–229. DOI: 10.1037/bul0000025.

[PubMed abstract and bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/)

Discussed in Chapter 8. Actual reading scope: the full public abstract and bibliographic information on PubMed, not full-text study. The text uses only the directional conclusion that monitoring interventions help goal attainment on average. Private records, choice of measures, and privacy boundaries are practical discussions in this book, not a uniform prescription directly derived from the meta-analysis.

## S4: Automaticity in Everyday Behavior

Lally, P., van Jaarsveld, C. H. M., Potts, H. W. W., & Wardle, J. (2010). How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world. *European Journal of Social Psychology, 40*(6), 998–1009. DOI: 10.1002/ejsp.674.

[Wiley abstract and bibliographic record](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)

Discussed in Chapter 10. Actual reading scope: the publisher's full public abstract and bibliographic information, not full-text study. The study concerned particular eating, drinking, or activity behaviors and self-reported automaticity. This book does not turn the model's time range into a habit-formation deadline for everyone, or extend the finding about one missed opportunity to a claim that any lengthy interruption is harmless.

## S5: Responding to Failure

Breines, J. G., & Chen, S. (2012). Self-compassion increases self-improvement motivation. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 38*(9), 1133–1143. DOI: 10.1177/0146167212445599.

[PubMed abstract and bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22645164/)

Discussed in Chapter 10. Actual reading scope: the full public abstract and bibliographic information on PubMed, not full-text study. Used to support the limited point that responding gently to failure can coexist with motivation to improve. It makes no guarantees about treatment effects, long-term change, or every situation involving procrastination.

## S6: Adjusting Goals

Wrosch, C., Scheier, M. F., Miller, G. E., Schulz, R., & Carver, C. S. (2003). Adaptive self-regulation of unattainable goals: Goal disengagement, goal reengagement, and subjective well-being. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 29*(12), 1494–1508. DOI: 10.1177/0146167203256921.

[PubMed abstract and bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15018681/)

Discussed in Chapter 13. Actual reading scope: the public PubMed abstract and bibliographic information returned in search results. A subsequent direct opening of the page did not return it in full; the paper was not studied in full text. The main text retains the language of association and does not use the study to decide which goal a reader should abandon. The specific exercises concerning leaving, pausing, and delivery boundaries were organized independently for this book.

## Influence on the Choice of Topic

James Clear. *Atomic Habits*. 2018. The commonly used Chinese title is 《掌控习惯》. This edition identifies the book only as an influence on the choice of topic. Its full text was not used as the source of this book's structure or arguments, and its chapters were not summarized, translated, rewritten, or matched chapter by chapter. Publication details for different language editions should be checked against the edition the reader actually uses.
