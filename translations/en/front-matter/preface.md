# Follow Through in Daily Life

KK

## Preface

This book is for people who have books they want to read, things they want to learn, and spaces they want to organize, alongside work, housework, caregiving, and unexpected demands they cannot ignore. You have no shortage of things you want to do. Yet at the end of the day, you often find that your own interests have once again been left for tomorrow.

We will begin with the circumstances of a particular attempt: where did things stop? From there, we will discuss capacity, starting, surroundings, attention, learning, feedback, interruptions, and endings. The aim is not to turn every spare moment into output. It is to give the things you have chosen, and genuinely consider worth doing, a place that works in your actual life.

Lin Yue, Zhou Hang, Chen Jing, and their experiences are original fictional illustrations. They do not represent the author's personal experiences, real interviews, or research cases. Their lives undergo no dramatic transformation, and their approaches change as their circumstances change. You can use their stories to think about your own situation without copying their arrangements.

The exercises in each chapter are suggestions for everyday practice. You may adapt, skip, or stop them; you do not need to complete them all. Examples of times and quantities illustrate possible arrangements, not universal standards. This book discusses following through in ordinary life. It does not provide medical diagnoses, psychotherapy, or disease-management plans, and should not replace appropriate professional help.

## About the Writing and Sources

KK initiated this book, with AI assistance in drafting, research, and editing. The Chinese manuscript is currently a complete first draft, and corrections and reader feedback are welcome. The author credit does not mean the situations described are KK's personal experiences or that every practical suggestion has been independently tested in an experiment.

James Clear's *Atomic Habits* was one influence on the choice of topic. This book is not a translation, abridgment, chapter-by-chapter retelling, or adaptation of that work, and claims no collaboration with or endorsement by its author or publisher. The manuscript is independently organized around practical problems in everyday action; individual studies are listed separately. This edition does not offer a comprehensive assessment of *Atomic Habits* based on reading the entire book.

Passages discussing research include source links. The notes at the end explain the actual reading scope for each source. For this edition, source checking primarily covered publicly available abstracts and bibliographic information for original papers or meta-analyses. Reading abstracts is not described as studying full papers. Any reasoning that connects research findings with this book's everyday suggestions is the responsibility of this book; it is not presented as a promise made by the researchers.

You can start with Chapter 1 or go straight to the problem you face. If you are already very busy, begin with Chapter 2. If you have just experienced an interruption, try Chapter 10. If you are unsure whether to continue, try Chapter 13. Wherever you begin, you need take away only one change you can use now.
